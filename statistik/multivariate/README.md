Chapter nine. The block exists to say two things precisely: the margins do
not determine the joint distribution, and the covariance term in the variance
of a sum is not optional.

The example that carries the block is the symmetric one: a variable and its
square, with covariance exactly zero and complete dependence. Independence
implies uncorrelated, uncorrelated does not imply independent, and every
statement about variances adding rests on the weaker of the two, which is why
it is worth knowing which one a given argument actually needs.
