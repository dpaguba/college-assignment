Chapter ten, where every statement is about a procedure rather than about a
number, and is therefore measurable by repeating the procedure. That is what
this block does: the bias of the uncorrected variance comes out at -0.565
against a theoretical -0.5625, the coverage of a nominal 95 percent interval
comes out at 0.91 for small samples, and the rejection rate of a level 0.05
test comes out at 0.05.

Two of those measurements are also warnings. The interval built with a normal
quantile under-covers by four points at n = 10, which is what the t
distribution exists to fix. The sign test cannot reject at five percent with
five observations, whatever the data looks like, because the smallest
possible p value is 0.0625.
