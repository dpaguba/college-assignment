# Transformations

Applying a function to a random variable gives a new one, and its
distribution is obtained by collecting the probability of every preimage.
That collection is the whole content: a transformation that is not injective
merges probabilities, so the die of the fifth sheet taken modulo 3 gives the
value 0 a probability of one half, being the sum of the probabilities of 3
and 6.

Standardising, subtracting the expectation and dividing by the standard
deviation, gives mean zero and variance one for any variable with positive
spread. That is what makes the normal table usable for every normal
distribution and what the central limit theorem is stated in terms of.

The indicator of an event is the simplest transformation, and its expectation
is the probability of the event. It is the bridge between the language of
events from the previous block and the language of random variables in this
one, and it is why a proportion can be treated as a mean.
