# Location

Three answers to "where is the middle", on the exam data of the first sheet,
`4, 2, 2, 1, 2, 3, 4, 2, 3, 2`:

| | |
|---|---|
| mode | 2 (five times) |
| median | 2 |
| mean | 2.5 |

All three are reproduced from the published solution. They differ here by
half a unit and differ far more once a single extreme value is added: the
median of `1, 2, 3, 4, 5` and of `1, 2, 3, 4, 500` is the same, and the mean
moves by 99.

## Which mean

The arithmetic mean is the balance point and the right average for quantities
that add. Rates that multiply need the geometric mean: doubling and then
quartering is a factor of one half overall, and the geometric mean of 2 and
0.25 gives 0.707 while the arithmetic mean gives 1.125, which corresponds to
no sequence of events at all.

The mode is the only one of the three that survives on nominal data, which is
why it is listed first in the scale table even though it is the least
informative.
