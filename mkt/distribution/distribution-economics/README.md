# The arithmetic

## Margins multiply

Each stage marks up the price it pays, not the manufacturer's price. Two
stages at thirty percent do not give sixty but 104: 100 € becomes 142.86 €
becomes 204.08 €. Adding the margins would give 160 €, and the 44 € gap is
where the manufacturer's picture of the shelf price goes wrong.

The chain inverts, which is the check: working back from 204.08 € through the
same two margins returns 100 €.

## The threshold for going direct

Own distribution costs a fixed amount and saves the trade margin on every
unit, so the threshold is the fixed cost divided by the saving per unit. At
200 000 € fixed, a 20 € unit margin and a thirty percent trade share, it is
33 333 units.

## What the calculation leaves out

It compares the same volume on both routes, and that is exactly the assumption
that fails. The trade brings reach; without it the volume falls, often by more
than the saved margin is worth. The right question is therefore not whether
direct distribution is cheaper but at what volume it would be, and whether
that volume is reachable without the trade.
