# Market research

Four methods with what they yield and what they risk: a survey gets opinions
and intentions and risks that saying is not doing; observation gets actual
behaviour and leaves the why open; an experiment gets a causal statement in an
artificial setting; a panel gets change over time from the same people, at the
price of attrition and habituation.

## Scales

Nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio. A mean is meaningless on an ordinal scale,
because the distance between ranks is unknown. School grades are averaged
everywhere anyway, and the lecture names it as a mistake that has become a
convention.

## The square root law

The sampling error falls with the square root of the sample size, so halving
the margin needs four times the sample:

| Sample | Margin |
|---:|---:|
| 250 | 6.20 % |
| 1 000 | 3.10 % |
| 4 000 | 1.55 % |
| 16 000 | 0.78 % |

And it does not depend on the size of the population as long as that is large
against the sample, which surprises people reliably and is why a thousand
respondents suffice for a country. Three percentage points need 1 068.

## The expensive mistake

Mistaking intention for behaviour. Respondents say reliably that they would
buy a new product, and then do not.
