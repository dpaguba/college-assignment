# Price floors

The short-run floor is the variable unit cost: above it every unit sold
contributes something to fixed costs that would be incurred anyway. The
long-run floor is full cost, because over time the fixed costs have to be
earned too.

An additional order is judged against what it actually adds: order-specific
fixed costs count, general fixed costs do not. Four hundred units at 10 € with
variable costs of 8 € and 500 € of setup contribute 300 € and are worth
taking; a hundred units on the same terms are not.

## Why the short-run floor is dangerous

It is arithmetically right and practically risky. The low price becomes the
customer's reference price and does not go away; the firm's own full-paying
customers find their way into the same channel; and what was meant as an
exception becomes the rule as soon as capacity depends on it. Short-run means
short-run, and somebody has to enforce that.

## Cost-plus pricing

The most common method and the one that never mentions the market. Its
weakness is in the denominator: the quantity depends on the price being
computed. If volume falls the price rises, and then volume falls further.
