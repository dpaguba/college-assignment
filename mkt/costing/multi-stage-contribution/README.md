# Contribution margin, several stages

Fixed costs are not allocated; they are subtracted where they arise. Area
fixed costs at the area, the rest at the company. Every figure then answers a
question of the form "what would fall away if this fell away", which is what
makes it usable for a decision.

| Stage | A | B |
|---|---:|---:|
| contributions of the products | 31 905 € | 6 500 € |
| less the area's fixed costs | 12 000 € | 7 000 € |
| contribution of the area | **19 905 €** | **−500 €** |

Company contribution 19 405 €, company fixed costs 6 000 €, operating result
**13 405 €**.

## What only this version shows

The operating result is identical to the single-stage figure; it has to be,
since the same fixed costs are subtracted, only elsewhere. The difference is
what becomes visible in between: area B does not cover its own fixed costs.
The single-stage calculation cannot say that, because it never asks.

## When it cannot steer

It assumes a product's contribution can be changed independently of the
others. That fails when products depend on each other, when one completes the
range, when a bottleneck ties the quantities together, or when the fixed costs
turn out to move after all. The last is the most common: what is fixed over a
year is not fixed over three.
