# Contribution margin, one stage

Add every product's contribution, subtract all the fixed costs at once. The
result is right and says nothing about where the money is earned.

| Product | Quantity | Price | Variable | Contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Standmixer | 300 | 40 € | 24 € | 4 800 € |
| Stabmixer | 405 | 50 € | 29 € | 8 505 € |
| Universalzerkleinerer | 200 | 100 € | 7 € | 18 600 € |
| Handrührer | 100 | 30 € | 37 € | **−700 €** |
| Küchenmaschine | 150 | 90 € | 42 € | 7 200 € |

Sum 38 405 €, less 25 000 € fixed, operating result **13 405 €**.

## The death spiral, measured

Allocate the fixed costs by volume and three of the five products show a loss
under full costing: Standmixer, Stabmixer and Handrührer. Drop those three and
the operating result falls from 13 405 € to **800 €**, because their
contributions disappear and the fixed costs do not. The survivors then carry
the whole burden, the calculation repeats, and the name for it is the death
spiral.

Only the Handrührer should actually go, and for a different reason: its
contribution is negative per unit, before any allocation.
