# Break-even

From `Kf + kv·x = p·x` follows `x = Kf/(p − kv)`. A target profit joins the
fixed costs, because the firm has to earn it just as surely.

## The exercise

"Daniel's Clear": fixed 500 000 €, variable 4 €, price 14 €.

| | |
|---|---:|
| unit contribution | 10 € |
| break-even quantity | 50 000 |
| for a profit of 600 000 € | 110 000 |

## What moves it most

A ten percent rise in fixed costs raises the critical quantity by exactly ten
percent, because they sit in the numerator. A ten percent price cut raises it
by sixteen, because the price sits in the denominator through the
contribution. The price is the sensitive input, and it is also the one most
readily conceded in a negotiation.

## The assumptions

One price regardless of quantity, variable costs linear in quantity, fixed
costs genuinely fixed over the range, and one product. Volume discounts break
the first, learning curves the second, a second shift the third, and with
several products the question of whose fixed costs these are has to be settled
first.
