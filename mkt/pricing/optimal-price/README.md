# The optimal price

With linear demand and constant unit cost, the profit-maximising price is
halfway between the unit cost and the prohibitive price: `p* = (a/b + k)/2`.
The formula and a twenty-thousand-step grid search agree at every cost tested.

## Fixed costs do not appear

They are a constant in the profit and vanish on differentiation. Measured: at
fixed costs of 0, 5 000 and 50 000 € the grid search finds the same price of
30 € and three different profits.

Raising a price because the rent went up therefore works against the
arithmetic. The rent changes whether the business is worth running, not how
dearly it should sell.

## Amoroso-Robinson

At the optimum, `p = k / (1 + 1/ε)`. Two things follow. The optimal price is
always in the elastic range, and the less elastic the demand, the larger the
mark-up over cost. With zero unit cost the elasticity at the optimum is
exactly minus one, which ties this module back to the revenue peak in the
previous one.

## What the formula assumes

A known demand function, no competitor who reacts, a product with no
neighbours in the range, and customers who do not read the price as a quality
signal. Each of those assumptions is precisely a question the rest of the
subject is about.
