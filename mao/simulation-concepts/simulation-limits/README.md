# Possibilities and limits

The ninth sheet asks for three advantages, three disadvantages and three
typical mistakes, and its published solution lists them. They are reproduced
here as data rather than prose.

The advantages come down to one: systems that no analytical model describes
can be studied, under conditions that may be real or impossible, in
timescales that cannot be observed.

The disadvantages come down to another: a stochastic simulation produces a
stochastic answer, needs a great deal of data, and produces enough output for
its accuracy to be overestimated.

The mistake worth putting first is the solution's: **means instead of
distributions**. A simulation fed with average values produces an average
answer and hides exactly the variability it was built to study, and it does
so silently, because the output looks like a result.
