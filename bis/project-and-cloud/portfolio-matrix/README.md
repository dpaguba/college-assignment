# The IT project portfolio matrix

Utility upwards, risk to the left, and the segments are quarter circles
around the bottom-left corner, which is high risk at low utility. The further
from that corner, the better the project.

- **Segment III:** high utility and low risk. Approved first.
- **Segment II:** middling on one of the two. Has to be prioritised, because
  these compete for the same resources.
- **Segment I:** high risk and low utility. May never be realised.

## The five projects of the exercise

Risk is the mean of a project's risk scores, utility the mean of its
strategic fit.

| Project | Risk | Utility | Distance | Segment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| P1 Kundenportal | 3.50 | 8.00 | 0.729 | III |
| P4 Neue Fassade | 3.33 | 7.00 | 0.684 | III |
| P2 KI-Prognose | 6.50 | 9.00 | 0.683 | III |
| P5 IoT Produkt | 8.00 | 8.33 | 0.606 | II |
| P3 ERP-Migration | 5.83 | 6.00 | 0.517 | II |

Two things are worth reading off. **Segment I is empty:** this portfolio
contains no project that is both risky and pointless, so the decision is not
what to drop but what to do first, and the third dimension the picture
carries as bubble size, the budget, is what decides that.

And **P4 and P2 are separated by one thousandth.** That gap is not a finding.
It says the two are equivalent on these two criteria, and anything that
distinguishes them has to come from somewhere else.
