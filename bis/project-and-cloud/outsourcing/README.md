# IT outsourcing

Three external sources: buying software from a vendor, using it online as a
service, or handing bespoke development to an external shop.

The advantages the lecture lists are cost saving, risk reduction,
concentration on core competences, avoided investment, and access to know-how
and experts. The disadvantages are dependency on the provider, giving away
knowledge, loss of control and the cost of controlling, quality problems,
resistance among staff, and the risk of relocating a problem rather than
solving it.

That last one is the quietest. A process that is poorly managed does not
become better because somebody else executes it; it becomes poorly managed at
arm's length.

## The break-even

`break_even` compares a service run in-house, which has a fixed cost plus a
rate per unit, against a provider that charges only per unit. Below the
threshold the provider is cheaper, above it the in-house option. At 100 000 €
fixed, 5 € internal and 9 € external per unit, the threshold is 25 000 units.

## What the sum leaves out

Managing the provider, contracts, acceptance, escalation, the knowledge that
disappears in-house once nobody practises it, and the cost of switching
later. All of them arrive after the saving does, which is why the sum tends
to look better at the decision than in the third year.
