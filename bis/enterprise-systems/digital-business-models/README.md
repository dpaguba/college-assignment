# Digital business models

Components: what the customer gets and why it helps them, how the service is
produced and by whom, and where the money comes from, plus the segment and
the channels.

Patterns: subscription, freemium, platform, long tail, pay per use,
advertising.

## What makes a model digital

Not the technology, but the cost structure: marginal cost near zero against
high fixed cost. Everything else follows. Scaling without unit cost follows,
subscription instead of single sale follows, and so does the tendency towards
a few large providers.

## Network effects

`network_effect` counts what a network makes possible. One-sided, all
participants can connect to each other: n(n−1)/2 pairs, so a hundred users
give 4950 possible connections and the value grows roughly with the square.
Two-sided, only connections across the sides count: a hundred users and ten
providers give a thousand.

Both explain the same thing. A platform is worth almost nothing at the start
and very hard to catch past a certain size, which is why the interesting
question is the chicken-and-egg one: each side waits for the other, and the
ways out are subsidising a side, starting in a niche where few participants
suffice, or supplying one side yourself until the other carries.
