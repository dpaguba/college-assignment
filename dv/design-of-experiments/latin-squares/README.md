# Latin squares

Every symbol once in every row and every column. Counted by exhaustive
enumeration: 12 squares of order 3 and 576 of order 4, matching the known
values, with 1 and 4 reduced squares respectively.

## The concentration experiment

Three factors with three levels each: sleep, coffee, morning exercise. Fully
crossed that is 27 groups; the exercise has nine. A Latin square puts the
third factor as the symbol inside the square spanned by the first two, and
nine runs suffice.

Every pair of levels from any two factors occurs exactly once, which the
module verifies for all three pairs of factors.

## What it costs

Exactly that property is the problem. Since each pair of levels appears once
and only once, the effect of a combination cannot be separated from the sum
of the individual effects. The plan assumes there are no interactions; if
there are, they land on top of the main effects and nothing in the data can
tell them apart.

A Graeco-Latin square adds a fourth factor by laying a second, orthogonal
square over the first. It exists for every order except two.
