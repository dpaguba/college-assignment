# The process portfolio

Which process to improve first. Three criteria:

- **strategic importance**, judged by management;
- **need for improvement**, the gap between target and actual figures;
- **ability to improve**, judged by the process owners.

## Why the score is a product

`score` multiplies the three rather than adding them. They do not substitute
for each other: a process that cannot be improved is worth nothing to the
programme however important it is and however badly it performs. A sum would
let two high values cover a zero in the third.

## The exercise

Six processes of a university. The selection focus is the upper-left field,
high importance and high need; the ability decides the order inside it, not
the entry.

| Process | Importance | Need | Ability | In focus |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Kurse ansetzen | 95 % | 85 % | 50 % | yes, first |
| Studierendenberatung verwalten | 85 % | 50 % | 40 % | yes |
| Kurse durchführen | 95 % | 30 % | 30 % | yes |
| Studienprogramme vermarkten | 75 % | 20 % | 60 % | no, need too low |
| Studienprogramme entwickeln und verwalten | 90 % | 10 % | 40 % | no, need too low |
| Räume verwalten | 35 % | 75 % | 70 % | no, not strategic |

Managing rooms is the interesting row: it scores well on two of three
criteria and is still not picked, because the one it fails is the one that
decides whether anybody cares about the result.
