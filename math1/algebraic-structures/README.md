The second half of the second part of the lecture, built by adding one
condition at a time. Of the 19 683 binary operations on a three-element set,
113 are associative, which is the measured version of the claim that
associativity is a strong requirement.

Two structural results run through the block in parallel. A quotient group
exists exactly for a normal subgroup, and a quotient ring exactly for an
ideal, and in both cases the condition is well-definedness of the operation
on classes rather than anything about the classes themselves. The
homomorphism theorem then says every quotient is an image and every image is
a quotient, which is checked here by constructing the isomorphism.

The result most worth remembering is the one that refuses to generalise: Q8
is not abelian and all six of its subgroups are normal, so normality is not a
weak form of commutativity.
