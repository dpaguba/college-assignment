# Ideals

An ideal is an additive subgroup that absorbs multiplication by the whole
ring, and that absorption is exactly what makes the quotient a ring, in the
same way that normality makes a quotient group.

In the residues modulo 12 there are six ideals and 12 has six divisors, which
is not a coincidence: every ideal is generated by a single element, and the
generator can be taken to be a divisor. The tests check that each of the six
is principal rather than assuming it.

## Closure properties

The intersection of two ideals is an ideal and so is their sum, both checked
over every pair of the six. Those two facts are what make "the ideal
generated by a set" well defined, since it is the intersection of all ideals
containing the set, and the intersection is guaranteed to be one.

The quotient of Z/12 by the ideal {0, 4, 8} has four elements and satisfies
the ring axioms, which is Z/4 arriving by a different route than reducing
integers modulo 4.
