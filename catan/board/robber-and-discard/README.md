# The seven

Two rules in one roll: everybody over seven cards discards half, rounded down,
and one player moves the robber and steals.

## The eighth card costs four

| hand | discarded |
|---:|---:|
| 7 | 0 |
| 8 | **4** |
| 9 | 4 |

The threshold is sharp. Taking an eighth card is not worth one card, it is
worth minus three, and that is why experienced players go into the roll
holding exactly seven and would rather build something suboptimal than accept
the eighth.

## The robber takes the whole hex, not a share

A robber on an 8 costs each adjacent settlement 5/36 of a card per turn, five
times what it costs on a 2. That is why he is placed on the strongest number
and not on the weakest opponent's most convenient hex.

## Why the seven is the balancing rule

One half of it is random and punishes hoarding. The other half is chosen by a
player and punishes whoever is ahead. Together they make a lead more expensive
the larger it is, and that is what keeps a game of Catan open long after the
positions have diverged.
