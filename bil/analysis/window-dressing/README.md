# Window dressing

What can be moved shortly before the closing date, and what only looks moved.

| Measure | Ratio it improves | What actually changed |
|---|---|---|
| sale and lease back | equity ratio, liquidity | nothing, the asset is still in use |
| factoring | liquidity, turnover | the fee lowers the result |
| deferring payments | the cash balance on the day | in January it is gone again |
| releasing provisions | result and equity ratio | the reason was gone before, or is not |
| changing depreciation | this year's result | the total stays |

## Two measures, computed

**Sale and lease back.** An asset with a book value of 100 000 € sold for
150 000 €: fixed assets fall, cash rises, and the 50 000 € gain raises equity.
The equity ratio goes from 40.0 % to 42.9 % and the company uses the same
machine as before. The rent arrives later, year after year.

**Factoring.** 100 000 € of receivables sold at a 2 % fee. The first degree of
liquidity goes from 0.20 to 0.59, which is the effect that was wanted. The
balance sheet shrinks by the 2 000 € fee, and so does equity, so the equity
ratio slips from 40.00 % to 39.88 %. The measure buys one ratio and costs
another, which is the part that is usually left out.

## What a reader looks for

A cluster of transactions shortly before the closing date, a jump in a ratio
without a jump in the business, a change of method in the notes, and a growing
gap between result and operating cash flow. None of them proves anything.
Together they say where to look more closely.

## The line

Bilanzpolitik uses the room the law leaves and is permitted. Bilanzfälschung
invents transactions or conceals them. The distinction is sharp in principle
and hard to prove in a single case, because both arrive as the same numbers,
and only the document and the intent separate them.
