# Year-end closing

Seven steps, in this order: inventory count, closing entries, income and
expense accounts into the profit and loss account, the profit and loss account
into equity, balance-sheet accounts into the Schlussbilanzkonto, the balance
sheet itself, and the opening account in the new year.

## Why the count comes first

Bookkeeping carries forward what the balances **should** be; the count
establishes what they **are**. Only the comparison shows shrinkage, breakage
and mis-postings. Turning the order round writes the bookkeeping into the
balance sheet and never checks it.

A shortfall is booked as expense, a surplus as income. A surplus is the more
suspicious of the two, because it usually means a booking was forgotten rather
than that goods appeared.

## Two roads to the same result

Equity at the end is equity at the start plus the result plus contributions
minus withdrawals. So the result can be had without the profit and loss
account, and the two figures must agree: starting at 100 000 € with 80 000 €
income, 60 000 € expense and a 5 000 € withdrawal gives a result of 20 000 €
and closing equity of 115 000 € both ways. A contribution raises equity
without being profit, which is what the comparison has to keep apart.

## The mirror

The opening account of the new year is the closing account read on the other
side: what stood in the credit column stands in the debit column. Mirroring
twice returns the original, which is the test the module runs. The tutorial
sheet states the relation the wrong way round: the opening account mirrors the
closing account of the **previous** year, not of the following one.
