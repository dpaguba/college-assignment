# Lower of cost or market

The asset side takes the lower value, the liability side the higher. Both
follow one rule: do not show yourself richer than you are.

| Case | Rule | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| current assets | strict lower value | every fall counts |
| fixed assets | moderate lower value | only a permanent fall counts |
| liabilities | highest value | a debt is not talked down |

## The asymmetry is deliberate

An unrealised loss of 30 is booked, an unrealised gain of 30 is not. That is
the Imparitätsprinzip and it exists to protect creditors, not because gains
are less certain than losses.

The other half is rarely said in the same breath: hidden reserves accumulate
systematically, the reported equity is too low, and every ratio built on it is
distorted in a known direction. An asset carried at 100 with a market value of
130 holds a hidden reserve of 30 that appears nowhere in the balance sheet.

## The cap on the way back

When the reason for a write-down falls away, the value must be written back
up, but never above cost. Written down from 100 to 70 and recovered to 130,
the value returns to 100 and stops: the acquisition cost is the ceiling of
every valuation, and a risen market price does not lift it.
