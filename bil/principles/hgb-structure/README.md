# The structure of the balance sheet

Assets are ordered by increasing liquidity, from intangibles to cash; the
capital side by decreasing maturity, from equity to short-term liabilities.
The order is the statement: how long the money is tied up, and how long it is
available.

## Size classes

| Class | Balance sheet | Revenue | Employees |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| klein | ≤ 6 000 000 € | ≤ 12 000 000 € | ≤ 50 |
| mittelgross | ≤ 20 000 000 € | ≤ 40 000 000 € | ≤ 250 |
| gross | above | above | above |

At least two of the three criteria, on two consecutive closing dates. A
company at 5 000 000 € with 10 000 000 € of revenue and 40 employees is small
on all three. One at 5 000 000 € with 60 000 000 € of revenue and 40 employees
is still small, because two of three suffice and the revenue alone does not
decide.

## What the class decides

The detail required in the balance sheet and the profit and loss account, the
scope of the notes, whether a management report is needed, whether the
accounts are audited, and how they are published. Small companies may present
an abridged balance sheet and skip the management report, which is why the
size class is the first thing to look up when accounts look unusually thin.
