# Principles of proper bookkeeping

The yardstick is § 238 HGB: an expert third party must be able to get an
overview of the transactions and the position of the company in reasonable
time. Not profit maximisation, not tax optimisation, and not internal control.
Comprehensibility for someone who was not there.

| Principle | What it demands |
|---|---|
| Richtigkeit und Willkürfreiheit | the entry matches the event and was not chosen |
| Klarheit und Übersichtlichkeit | the records are legible and ordered |
| Vollständigkeit | no transaction is missing |
| Einzelbewertung | each item is valued for itself |
| Vorsicht | in doubt, the less favourable assumption |
| Stetigkeit | the methods stay the same from year to year |
| Periodenabgrenzung | expense and income belong to their period |
| Going Concern | the company continues unless something says otherwise |

## No entry without a document

The rule serves verifiability. An entry that cannot be traced to a document
cannot be checked by anyone, and a set of records that cannot be checked has
no evidential value at all, including for the entries that happen to be
correct.

## Why consistency binds

A single set of accounts is barely interpretable. It becomes readable through
comparison with the previous year, and that comparison only works if the
methods did not move. A change is allowed, and it has to be disclosed and
justified, because without the note the reader compares two different things
and does not know it.
