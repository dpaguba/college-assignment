# Measurement principles

Three rules that decide when a figure enters the accounts and at what value.

**Realisation.** A gain appears when it is realised, that is, when the sale has
taken place. Held at cost 100 with a market value of 130 and unsold, the value
stays at 100 and the result is zero. Sold, the result is 30. The loss does not
wait: at a market value of 70, the value falls to 70 and the result is −30
before anything is sold.

**Closing date.** What counts is the position at the closing date. What happens
between the closing date and the day the accounts are drawn up is only taken
in as far as it says something about the position **at** the closing date. A
customer going bankrupt in February over a January invoice is new information
about an old receivable; a fire in February is a new event.

**Individual valuation.** Each item is valued for itself, and the losses are
not netted against the gains. Two items, one at 100 worth 130 and one at 100
worth 70, come to 170 item by item and 200 taken together. The 30 € difference
is the whole point: valued together, a loss would be covered by an unrealised
gain, and the caution would be lost.

## How they fit together

Caution stands above them, realisation and imparity are its two halves, and
individual valuation is what stops the imparity from being netted away. Remove
any one of the three and the other two lose their effect.
